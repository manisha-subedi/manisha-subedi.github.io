The chart still contains the same 40 dots. Only the grouping is now visible.
The overall association is negative, but the association within each group is
positive. This reversal is called Simpson's paradox.

## Why the overall line slopes down

Small cities spend about 30 euros per person and grow by about 3.5 percent.
Large cities spend about 75 euros per person and grow by less than 2 percent.
The simulation also gives large cities a lower starting growth rate.

A single line through all 40 cities mainly separates the two groups. Small
cities are on the left with higher growth. Large cities are on the right with
lower growth. The line therefore slopes down. It is comparing the small-city
cluster with the large-city cluster, not only comparing different levels of
marketing spend.

Within each group, the city-size difference is held fixed. The remaining
relationship is between spend and growth, and both group lines slope up.

The grey line answers one question: how are spend and growth associated
across all cities? The coloured lines answer another: how are they associated
within each city-size group? The answers have opposite directions because the
questions are different.

## Change the gap between the groups

The reversal depends on a third variable that is related to both axes. In
this example, city size is associated with higher marketing spend and lower
sales growth. Use the slider to make the two groups more similar. As they

## Reproduce the example in Python

```python
import numpy as np

small_spend  = np.random.uniform(10, 50, 20)
small_growth = 2 + 0.05 * small_spend + np.random.normal(0, 0.4, 20)

big_spend  = np.random.uniform(55, 95, 20)
big_growth = -2 + 0.05 * big_spend + np.random.normal(0, 0.4, 20)  # same slope, lower start

slope = lambda x, y: np.polyfit(x, y, 1)[0]
slope(small_spend, small_growth)   # about +0.05
slope(big_spend, big_growth)       # about +0.05
slope(np.concatenate([small_spend, big_spend]),
      np.concatenate([small_growth, big_growth]))   # negative
```

Both groups have a positive slope of about +0.05. The line through the
combined data has a negative slope because the groups start in different
places.

## Check grouped results before interpreting a trend

The same pattern can appear when data combine customer segments, countries,
device types, old and new customers, or weekdays and weekends.

Before interpreting one trend line, calculate or plot the relationship within
relevant groups. If the direction changes, report both the overall result and
the grouped results. The grouping variable belongs in the explanation.

A trend line describes an association between the plotted values. On its own,
it does not show whether marketing caused sales growth to rise or fall.
