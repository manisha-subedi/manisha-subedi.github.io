A tile in Power BI says: August revenue, 42,000. It looks like a fact. But
it is the end of a long trip. One row made that trip through eight stops. At
every stop, something about the row changed. Some changes were decisions.
Some were just defaults nobody looked at.

This post follows one order from the checkout to the tile. The data is made
up. Scroll through the eight stops. The blue chip on the line is the order.

<RowJourney />

## Walk one row back

When two dashboards do not agree, the cause is almost always one stop where
they made a different choice. Local time or UTC. Cents or euros. Paid orders
or all orders. The fastest way to find it is this: take one row, and walk it
back, stop by stop, until the two paths split.

The month of this order is a good example. The customer paid at 00:30 on
September 1, in Lisbon. The export changed the time to UTC, and in UTC it
was still August 31. So the warehouse says August. The app says September.
Both are correct for their own clock. The report has to say which clock it
uses. If it does not, the number is not wrong. But it is not a fact either.
