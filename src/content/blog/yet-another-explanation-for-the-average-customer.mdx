Average spending can be useful without describing what most customers
spend. Consider a small online shop with 30 customers: 29 spent 10 euros
each, and one wholesale buyer spent 1,000 euros.

**Q1. What is the total revenue?**

The 29 smaller purchases add up to 290 euros. Including the wholesale
order, revenue is 1,290 euros.

**Q2. What does the average customer spend?**

Divide 1,290 by 30 customers to get a mean of 43 euros.

**Q3. How many customers spent around 43 euros?**

None. Most spent 10 euros, while one spent 1,000. The mean is correct, but
it does not describe a typical purchase in this example.

**Q4. Next month, the wholesale buyer does not return. The other 29
customers spend the same amount. What is the average now?**

Revenue is 290 euros across 29 customers, so the mean falls to 10 euros.
That is a drop of about 77 percent, even though the remaining customers
have not changed their spending.

<MeanBalanceFigure />

**Q5. What happens if we use the middle value instead?**

Sort the 30 customers by spending. The middle two values, at positions 15
and 16, are both 10 euros, so the median is 10 euros. Remove the wholesale
buyer and the median remains 10 euros. Unlike the mean, it is not pulled
up by the size of that one purchase.

<MedianLineFigure />

**Q6. Your manager wants one number for the slide. Which one do you give?**

It depends on the question. For revenue per customer, use the mean of
43 euros. For typical customer spending, the median of 10 euros is more
helpful here.

If there is room for context, report both and explain the difference:
most customers spent 10 euros, while one wholesale order brought total
revenue to 1,290 euros.

## The same calculation in code

```python
spend = [10] * 29 + [1000]

mean = sum(spend) / len(spend)            # 43.0
median = sorted(spend)[len(spend) // 2]   # 10

without_whale = spend[:-1]
sum(without_whale) / len(without_whale)   # 10.0
sorted(without_whale)[len(without_whale) // 2]   # 10
```

## Using this in an analysis

Large values can have the same effect on salaries, delivery times, or page
load times. A few unusually high observations can pull the mean away from
what most people experience.

I would compare the mean and median before choosing a summary for a report.
If they differ substantially, it is worth looking at the distribution and
the largest values. Neither measure is automatically better; the choice
depends on what the report needs to explain.
