During my internship at CIMO, I worked with physicochemical measurements,
HPLC data, and two antioxidant assays. A result may look simple in a table,
but it comes from a sample, a method, a unit, and a set of conditions.

## Keep the context nearby

Two values can look comparable even when they came from different methods or
conditions. A spreadsheet will not warn you. Units, source, date, and any
transformation need to stay close to the result. If that context is missing,
the number needs another check before it becomes a chart.

An unusual result is not automatically an error. It may come from a
transcription mistake, a measurement issue, or a real difference in the
sample. Removing it because it looks awkward is not enough. The original
record needs to be checked.

A table can have no blank cells and still be wrong. The work before the chart
matters just as much as the chart itself.
